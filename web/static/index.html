ToxiTrace checks food proteins and contaminants against six public scientific databases and the food safety laws of three regions. Every figure it shows you names where it came from. Nothing is guessed, and nothing is written just because it would sound convincing.

What it is

It finds the evidence. It doesn't invent any.

Name a food protein and a contaminant. ToxiTrace finds both in public scientific databases, measures the contaminant's properties directly from its structure, pulls the published research on the pair, and checks any lab result you have against the legal limits in the EU, US and India.

What comes back is a report you can audit. Each figure names the database it came from, the record ID, and when it was fetched — so you can open the original and confirm it yourself.

What it won't do

  • No predicted binding strengths. Getting these right needs specialist software and lab confirmation. A made-up number is worse than none.
  • No single risk score. You can check a measurement against a published limit yourself. You can't check a number out of ten.
  • It won't fill a gap. When a database returns nothing, the report says nothing was found. That's a result too.

How it works

Six steps, in this order

The order matters. Each step needs what the one before it produced — there's no structure to fetch until the protein has been identified, and nothing to check until the figures exist.

  1. 01

    Resolve

    The protein name you typed is matched to a curated UniProtKB entry. The contaminant is matched to its PubChem record and real chemical structure.

  2. 02

    Fetch

    The protein's AlphaFold structure is pulled from the public database, along with how confident the prediction is at every position.

  3. 03

    Compute

    Molecular weight, logP, TPSA and Lipinski violations are measured from the contaminant's own structure. The same molecule always gives the same numbers.

  4. 04

    Cite

    PubMed is searched for research on that exact pairing. You get the papers that exist, with their IDs — or a clear note that none were found.

  5. 05

    Compare

    Your lab result is set against the published maximum levels in the EU, US and India, each one naming the regulation it comes from.

  6. 06

    Verify

    Every figure and record number in the written summary is checked back against what was actually retrieved. Anything that doesn't match is flagged, not printed quietly.
